# Lusi Jewelry — Brand Strategy
Version 1.0 — Prepared for website design & development handoff

---

## 1. Company Snapshot

Lusi Jewelry is a high-end diamond and gold jewelry house founded in 2006, operating from a single retail atelier in Gyumri, Armenia. The business grew steadily until 2014, when Russian sanctions and ruble devaluation disrupted its core customer base (cross-border and diaspora buyers). Since then the business has been stable but not growing. The current initiative is to build a digital brand and e-commerce-ready website to (1) establish credibility beyond word-of-mouth, (2) generate online catalog/inquiry-based orders in Armenia, and (3) begin reaching the Armenian diaspora in the USA, EU, and Russia, where the brand can compete on a genuine price advantage against local Western jewelers.

Lusi designs and produces jewelry in-house, including custom/made-to-order pieces built from 3D models by an in-house designer — a real, defensible differentiator most local competitors cannot claim. Price range spans $200–$8,000, positioning the brand as accessible luxury with room to grow upward, not a mass-market costume jewelry seller.

**Name note:** "Lusi" is an Armenian name meaning "light" — a strong, literal metaphor already available for visual and verbal identity (brilliance, illumination, clarity) without needing to state it overtly.

---

## 2. Strategic Tension to Resolve (Read This First)

The stated aspiration is to be perceived like Cartier or Tiffany — heritage luxury houses. The honest constraint: those brands are built on decades of scarcity, five- and six-figure price floors, flagship real estate in Paris, London, and New York, and cultural mythology that cannot be manufactured in a single website sprint. Positioning Lusi as heritage luxury today, at a $200 price floor with one storefront in Gyumri, would create a credibility gap the moment a visitor compares the claim to the reality — the fastest way to lose the trust this project is trying to build.

**The resolution:** position Lusi as a **modern atelier brand** — the Blue Nile/Mejuri model. This model earns trust through transparency (certification, craftsmanship visibility, clear pricing), quality of design and photography, and consistency — not through claimed heritage status. It is honest about where the brand is today, while every visual and verbal choice in this kit is built to *scale upward* toward a more elevated, heritage-grade presentation as the brand grows. Think of this as heritage luxury's future self, reached the credible way — earned trust first, prestige later — rather than heritage luxury's costume, worn too early.

This decision governs everything downstream: tone of voice, photography style, page structure (certification and craftsmanship get prominent real estate), and even what is *not* said (see Section 5, Nationality Positioning).

## 3. Mission

**To make genuine, master-crafted diamond jewelry honestly accessible — bringing atelier-level design and certified quality to people who have been priced out of it, without pretense.**

## 4. Vision

**Within five years, Lusi Jewelry is a recognized name within the Armenian diaspora across the US, EU, and Russia — the trusted source for certified diamond jewelry that combines in-house craftsmanship with a genuine price advantage over local Western jewelers.**

This is deliberately narrower than "become a global luxury brand." A vision statement that overreaches is not a vision — it is a wish. Diaspora recognition is ambitious but achievable and gives every marketing and design decision a concrete audience to aim at.

## 5. Values

1. **Honesty over performance.** Certification is shown, not claimed. Photography shows the real product. No invented heritage myths.
2. **Craftsmanship as the core asset.** In-house design and production (including custom 3D-modeled pieces) is the brand's most defensible advantage and should be visible throughout the site, not buried in an About page.
3. **Accessible without being cheap.** Price transparency and value framing, never discount-driven or "sale" language.
4. **Quiet confidence.** The brand does not need to shout. Elegant restraint signals more trust than ornamentation.
5. **Consistency.** Every touchpoint — Instagram, website, packaging, customer service — should feel like the same brand said it.

## 6. Positioning Statement

For the Armenian diaspora and discerning local buyers who want certified, high-quality diamond jewelry without heritage-brand markup, Lusi Jewelry is the modern atelier that designs and crafts each piece in-house — unlike mass-market retailers or resellers, Lusi offers GIA-certified diamonds, custom design capability, and atelier pricing rooted in nearly two decades of hands-on craftsmanship.

## 7. Unique Selling Proposition (USP)

**In-house craftsmanship + certified diamonds + a genuine price advantage.**

Three pillars, none of which competitors (Pandora, Times Jewelry, or generic resellers) can claim together:
- **Made, not just sold.** Lusi has its own designers and production, including 3D custom design — most local competitors resell.
- **Certified trust.** GIA certification available on diamonds, addressing the #1 barrier to online jewelry purchases (trust in authenticity).
- **Price advantage for diaspora buyers.** Genuine cost savings versus US/EU jewelers for equivalent quality — this is a real, quantifiable argument, not a soft claim.

## 8. Target Audience

### Primary Segment 1 — Local Armenian Buyers (Existing Base)
Customers in Gyumri/Armenia, largely trust-based and referral-driven, buying for engagement, weddings, and gifting occasions. Values relationship and reputation; this segment is already loyal but does not currently support growth on its own.

### Primary Segment 2 — Armenian Diaspora (Growth Target)
Located in the US, EU, and Russia. Household income above roughly $3,000/month (indicating discretionary spending capacity for $200–$8,000 jewelry). Buying both as gifts sent home and for themselves. Primary emotional/rational driver is **value**: comparable or better quality than local Western jewelers, at a meaningfully lower price — the "why pay Western markup" argument. Engagement and bridal purchases are the leading use case for this segment; everyday luxury gifting is secondary. Do not market this segment on nationality/heritage nostalgia as the primary hook (see Section 5 below) — lead with product quality and price advantage, let the Armenian connection be a quiet, felt undertone rather than the pitch.

### Persona A — "The Diaspora Groom"
Male, 28–42, based in the US or EU, dual-income household, planning an engagement or wedding. Has priced rings at a Western jeweler and been surprised by the cost. Wants certification and confidence he isn't being scammed buying internationally. Needs: clear GIA certification info, transparent pricing, real customer proof (reviews, completed orders), simple international shipping/customs information, and a way to ask questions before committing (WhatsApp/inquiry, not just a cart).

### Persona B — "The Established Local Client"
Female or male, 35–55, based in Yerevan or Gyumri, existing or referred customer, higher discretionary spend, values relationship and craftsmanship story. Needs: an online presence that matches or exceeds the elegance of the in-store experience, custom/made-to-order visibility, and easy contact with the atelier.

### Persona C — "The Diaspora Gift Buyer"
Any gender, 30–60, buying a gift for a family member in Armenia (mother, sister, daughter) for a wedding, birthday, or holiday. Needs: an easy way to browse, understand sizing, and arrange delivery to Armenia — plus reassurance the seller is legitimate.

## 9. Brand Personality

If Lusi Jewelry were a person: **an elegant, highly intelligent, self-possessed woman who runs her own business — confident without needing to prove it, precise, warm in close relationships but reserved in public.**

Personality traits to design and write toward:
- Elegant, not decorative
- Confident, not boastful
- Precise, not fussy
- Warm in one-to-one moments (customer service, custom orders), composed in broadcast moments (website, ads)

Brand archetype reference: closer to **The Sovereign/Ruler** (mastery, quality, quiet authority) blended with **The Creator** (craftsmanship, made by hand/mind) — not the Lover archetype (romance-heavy, common in mass jewelry marketing) and not the Everyman (approachable-cheap, Pandora's territory).

## 10. Nationality Positioning

Do not market Lusi as "Armenian jewelry" or lead with heritage/nationality framing, per explicit direction — the brand should read as a credible international atelier, competing visually and tonally with any Western luxury brand. This does not mean hiding facts (the Gyumri atelier, the 2006 founding, and in-house craftsmanship can and should be told as an atelier story), but framing should stay in the register of "an independent atelier since 2006" rather than "an Armenian brand." Nationality is a quiet, implicit undertone for diaspora audiences who will recognize it themselves — never the headline.

## 11. Brand Voice

**Formal, elegant, and confident.** Specifically:

- Sentences are complete and considered — no fragments-for-effect, no excessive exclamation points, no emoji in primary copy (the current "You were born to sparkle ✨" bio is a good example of what to move away from).
- Speaks with authority about craftsmanship and certification without over-explaining or sounding defensive.
- Avoids: "sale," "deal," "cheap," "affordable" (use "accessible," "considered value," or price transparency instead), superlatives without backing ("the best," "the most beautiful") — let the product and certification carry the claim.
- Avoids overly ornate, flowery prose — elegance through restraint and precision, not adjectives stacked on adjectives.
- Second person, direct address ("you") is appropriate for product and CTA copy; first person plural ("we") for brand/about voice.

**Voice do/don't example:**
- Don't: "You were born to sparkle! ✨ Shop our stunning new collection today!"
- Do: "Each piece is designed and set by hand in our atelier, certified for the diamond within it. Explore the current collection."

## 12. Messaging Pillars

1. **Certified, not just claimed.** GIA certification available — lead with this on product and trust-focused pages.
2. **Made in-house.** Every piece begins with in-house design (including custom 3D modeling), not a supplier catalog.
3. **Since 2006.** Nearly two decades of continuous craftsmanship — atelier framing, not nationality framing.
4. **Real value.** Comparable quality to Western jewelers at a genuine price advantage — stated plainly, never as a "discount."
5. **Made for you.** Custom and made-to-order capability — a piece can be designed specifically for the customer, not just selected off a shelf.

## 13. Tagline Options

Primary recommendation:

> **Lusi Jewelry — Light, by design.**

Plays on the meaning of "Lusi" (light) without stating it outright, ties to diamonds/brilliance, and stays elegant and short. Alternates:

- **Crafted to be certain.** (leans into trust/certification)
- **Made by hand. Held for a lifetime.** (leans into craftsmanship)
- **Considered brilliance.** (leans into restraint + product)

Recommend testing the primary tagline with real customers before finalizing; do not treat this as locked without validation, since tone perception is subjective and worth confirming with the target diaspora audience specifically.

## 14. Elevator Pitch

"Lusi Jewelry is an independent atelier that has designed and hand-crafted diamond and gold jewelry since 2006. Every piece is made in-house — including fully custom, 3D-modeled designs — and diamonds are available with GIA certification, so customers know exactly what they're buying. For the Armenian diaspora across the US, EU, and Russia, Lusi offers the same certified quality as a Western jeweler at a genuine price advantage, backed by nearly twenty years of hands-on craftsmanship."

## 15. Open Business Questions (Not Design Decisions — Flagging for Owner)

These came up during discovery and affect strategy but are business decisions, not branding ones. Recommend resolving before or during build:

- **Entry-price product line:** Local market feedback suggests demand for lower-priced branded jewelry (cubic zirconia/artificial stone, similar to Pandora/Times price points). Consider whether Lusi ever introduces a secondary, clearly-labeled entry line — this is a merchandising decision with brand implications (must never be confused with the certified diamond line) but is out of scope for this kit unless confirmed.
- **Shipping/customs for international orders:** Diaspora buyers cited shipping as the second-largest trust barrier after authenticity. The website will need a clear, honest shipping/customs policy page even in phase one (inquiry-based, pre-checkout).
- **Yerevan expansion:** Mentioned as a future consideration (mall or retail location). Not required for the website build now, but the site's location/contact architecture should be built to add a second location later without restructuring.
