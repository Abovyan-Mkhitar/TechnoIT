How much does it cost to outsource a developer in 2026?

Aug 18, 2026 7 min read Hiring

If you've started pricing out a new hire, you already know the sticker shock: recruiting fees, salary, benefits, payroll tax, equipment, office space. Outsourcing a developer sidesteps most of that — but the hourly rate itself still needs to make sense. Here's what it actually costs, model by model.

Hourly rates by seniority

Rates vary by region, tech stack and how the agency vets its talent, but as a general benchmark:

  • Junior Developer — around $15/hr. Best for well-scoped, supervised work: bug fixes, small features, QA support.
  • Mid-Level Developer — around $25/hr. Can own a feature end-to-end with light oversight — the sweet spot for most product work.
  • Senior Developer — around $35/hr. System architecture, technical leadership, and the judgment calls that are expensive to get wrong.

For custom quotes or bulk hiring across a full team, most outsourcing partners (including us) will negotiate a blended rate rather than pricing every seat individually.

The four hiring models

Cost isn't just about the hourly number — it's about which engagement model fits the shape of your work.

  • Hourly — pay per hour for short-term or unpredictable-scope work. Maximum flexibility, least commitment.
  • Part-time — a dedicated developer for a fixed number of hours per week. Good for ongoing maintenance or a slow-burn roadmap.
  • Full-time — a long-term, dedicated team member at a monthly rate. Effectively an extension of your team without the payroll overhead.
  • Project-based — a fixed-cost team scoped to a defined deliverable. Predictable budget, but requires a clear spec upfront.

What actually moves the price

Two developers at the "same" seniority level can be priced 20-30% apart. The variables that matter most:

  • Tech stack rarity — a common React/Node developer costs less than a specialist in, say, embedded systems or a niche ML framework.
  • Vetting depth — agencies that run real technical assessments and coding tests (not just a CV screen) charge more because the failure rate downstream is lower.
  • Region — timezone overlap and local cost of living both factor into the rate.
  • Contract structure — a trial period, month-to-month flexibility and no long-term lock-in are worth paying a small premium for if you're not yet sure of scope.

Outsourcing vs hiring in-house

An in-house mid-level developer's advertised salary is only part of the real cost. Once you add payroll tax, benefits, recruiting fees, onboarding time and office overhead, the fully-loaded cost typically runs 1.5-2x the base salary. Outsourcing collapses all of that into a single hourly or monthly rate — no contracts, payroll or compliance to manage on your end. We go deeper on this trade-off in In-House vs Outsourced Development Team.

Example budget scenarios

To make the numbers concrete, here's what a few common situations actually cost at outsourced rates:

  • A 3-month MVP build, one mid-level developer full-time (~160 hrs/month) — roughly $4,000/month, or $12,000 for the full build.
  • Ongoing part-time maintenance, one mid-level developer at 10 hrs/week — around $1,000/month, well below the cost of a part-time in-house hire once benefits and overhead are counted.
  • A short, well-scoped feature built by a senior developer over two weeks (~60 hrs) — roughly $2,100, delivered on a project-based, fixed-cost basis.

These are benchmarks, not quotes — actual numbers depend on stack, scope and how fast you need to move. But they're a reasonable starting point for budgeting before you talk to anyone.
